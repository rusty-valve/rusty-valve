# Why we built Rusty Valve

I trained in landscape architecture and brownfield remediation at Harvard, then spent years on chemical sites in China: solid waste, toxic wastewater, process optimization. Construction, commissioning, production.

On one pilot project treating pyridine wastewater, a bottom valve on a fixed bed was set too narrow. Feed kept coming in faster than it drained, and while I was away the bed overflowed at the inlet flange. I put on a mask, stopped the feed pump, and reached in for the bottom valve, which was hard to get to. Wastewater from above ran down onto my clothes, and the smell stayed for days.

Next door was a hydrogen fluoride unit. People went in fully suited. An operator told me every flange in there leaked, and that some workers had permanent burn marks on their faces.

None of this was unusual. That is the point. Somebody still has to walk up to the valve.

Robots are being trained in clean labs and tidy kitchens. We want to train them for the places where the work actually hurts people, starting with the valve.
